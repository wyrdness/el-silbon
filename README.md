## Overview

El Silbón ('the whistler') is a legendary spirit of the Llanos region of Venezuela — particularly Portuguesa state and Guanarito Municipality — and of Colombia's Eastern Llanos, said to have arisen as a folk legend in the mid-19th century. According to the most widely told version, El Silbón was a spoiled son who, in a drunken rage, killed his father, and (in some versions) prepared the body as food for his unsuspecting mother; on discovering the truth, his grandfather whipped him, rubbed lemon and pepper into his wounds, and cursed him to wander forever, carrying his father's bones in a sack. He appears as the shadow of a tall, gaunt man, sometimes wearing a hat; in some accounts as a six-metre giant moving among the treetops. His characteristic whistle is held to be inverted in distance: when it sounds near, he is far; when it sounds faint and distant, he is dangerously close. He preys particularly on drunkards and unfaithful husbands. Folkloric protections include a barking dog (the only thing he reportedly fears), chili peppers, and a whip — items linked to the curse imposed by his grandfather.

- **Origin**: Spanish 'el silbón', literally 'the whistler' or 'the one who whistles', from the verb 'silbar' (to whistle).

- **First use**: Recorded in 19th-century Llanero oral tradition; widely standardised in 20th-century Venezuelan folkloric literature.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Cursed unredeemed soul** _(Llanero folk-Catholicism)_

El Silbón is the soul of a real or archetypal patricide condemned to wander forever as punishment, in keeping with Catholic and Llanero conceptions of the unredeemed dead.

### Skeptical

**Moral-pedagogical folklore** _(Modern Venezuelan folkloristics)_

The legend functions as moral pedagogy and social control, warning especially against patricide, drunkenness, and infidelity in a rural Llanero context.

**Acoustic explanation of the inverted whistle** _(Local interpretive tradition)_

The 'reversed' whistle motif may encode a real acoustic phenomenon of the open Llanos — sounds carry unevenly across the savanna at night, particularly with temperature inversions.
